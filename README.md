# Bizz Tracker

A lightweight business-management prototype for small businesses that
need a simple way to track inventory, daily sales, and gross profit.

> **Project status:** Early frontend prototype. Data is stored in the
> browser using `localStorage`. This version is suitable for
> experimentation and demonstrations, not yet for managing critical
> business records.

## 1. Project overview

Bizz Tracker is designed for small retailers such as kiosks,
mini-markets, cosmetics shops, clothing stores, and electronics shops.

The initial prototype helps a business owner:

- Add products with buying price, selling price, stock quantity, and a
  low-stock threshold.
- View current inventory and stock status.
- Record sales against available stock.
- Automatically reduce stock after a sale.
- Calculate daily revenue and gross profit or loss.
- Review transactions recorded on the current day.
- Retain prototype data in the same browser using `localStorage`.

## 2. Current features

### Inventory

Each product stores:

Field Description

---

Product ID Unique identifier generated by the browser
Name Product name
Cost price Purchase cost per unit
Selling price Selling price per unit
Quantity Available units
Minimum stock Threshold used for low-stock warnings

Products are marked as **Available**, **Low stock**, or **Out of stock**
based on quantity and the configured minimum.

### Sales

A sale records the selected product, quantity sold, revenue, gross
profit, date, and time.

The application checks that:

- A product has been selected.
- The quantity is a positive whole number.
- Sufficient stock is available.

When a sale is accepted, the product's stock quantity is reduced.

### Dashboard

The dashboard displays:

- Today's revenue.
- Today's gross profit or loss.
- Total units currently in stock.
- Number of sales transactions today.
- Number of products at or below their low-stock threshold.

### Browser storage

The prototype stores data under these browser storage keys:

- `bizz_products`
- `bizz_sales`

Data remains available after refreshing the page in the same browser
profile, provided the browser's site data is not cleared.

2.  Create an `index.html` file inside it.

## 6. Business calculations

The prototype uses the following calculations.

### Revenue

```text
Revenue = Selling price per unit × Quantity sold
```

### Cost of goods sold

```text
Cost of goods sold = Buying cost per unit × Quantity sold
```

### Gross profit or loss

```text
Gross profit/loss = Revenue − Cost of goods sold
```

A negative result represents a gross loss on the sale.

### Example

A shop buys 10 bottles at KSh 80 each and sells them at KSh 120 each.

Metric Amount

---

Units purchased 10
Cost per unit KSh 80
Selling price per unit KSh 120
Total purchase cost KSh 800
Revenue if all 10 sell KSh 1,200
Gross profit if all 10 sell KSh 400

If 3 bottles are sold:

- Revenue: `3 × 120 = KSh 360`
- Cost of goods sold: `3 × 80 = KSh 240`
- Gross profit: `360 − 240 = KSh 120`
- Remaining stock: `10 − 3 = 7`

**Important:** Gross profit is not net profit. Net profit requires
accounting for operating expenses such as rent, electricity, transport,
wages, and other business costs. Expense tracking is a planned next
feature.

## 8. Current limitations

The prototype is intentionally small and has important limitations:

1.  **Local-only data:** Data is tied to one browser profile and device.
2.  **No user accounts:** There is no login, registration, or access
    control.
3.  **No multi-business support:** Businesses cannot maintain separate
    accounts.
4.  **No cloud backup:** Clearing browser data can permanently remove
    records.
5.  **No expense tracking:** The dashboard reports gross profit, not net
    profit.
6.  **No purchase history:** Initial stock is entered directly; stock
    replenishment is not yet recorded as a transaction.
7.  **No audit trail:** Changes and deletions are not recorded in an
    immutable history.
8.  **No concurrency protection:** Multiple users cannot safely update
    shared stock.
9.  **No formal accounting:** Taxes, discounts, refunds, credit sales,
    and payment reconciliation are not implemented.
10. **Date handling:** The current prototype uses the browser's local
    date and time. A production system should define and consistently
    enforce the business timezone.

Do not rely on this prototype as the only record of real business
transactions.

## 10. Production architecture considerations

A production sale should be handled as one database transaction:

1.  Authenticate the user and identify their business.
2.  Verify the product belongs to that business.
3.  Check that sufficient stock exists.
4.  Create the sale and its sale-item records.
5.  Reduce stock atomically.
6.  Commit the transaction.
7.  Return the saved transaction to the frontend.

If any step fails, the database transaction should roll back. This
prevents a sale from being recorded without the corresponding stock
update.

Every business-owned record should include a business identifier, and
the backend must enforce tenant isolation. Frontend filtering alone is
not a security boundary.

For reliable reporting, preserve the cost price used at the time of each
sale in the sale-item record. Do not calculate historical profit using
only the product's current cost price, because purchase costs can
change.

`sales` Sale headers and transaction

## 12. Development principles

- Keep business calculations consistent and testable.
- Validate input on both the frontend and backend.
- Never trust client-provided prices or totals in production.
- Use database transactions for stock and sales operations.
- Preserve historical sale prices and costs.
- Enforce business-level data access on the server.
- Back up business records and test restoration.
- Make important actions clear and recoverable.
